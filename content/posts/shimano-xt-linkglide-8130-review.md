My 2023 Trek Roscoe 7 came with Shimano Deore 6100 12spd. In that time, it seems like I was replacing chains every 300-400 miles. I don't recall replacing chains as often when I was using 9spd or 10spd drivetrains. After replacing the last chain, I knew it was close to time to change the cassette. So I started researching if I wanted to even stay with 12spd or what alternatives existed. I like nice bike parts; however, I also realize I'm extremely practical. Mid-level drivetrains have always made a lot of sense to me considering they wear out and can get easily damaged. So I was curious about Shimano CUES and MicroShift Advent MX as my next drivetrain. Well then it happened; I didn't make it to being worn out. I was coming down my local trails when I heard a thump, which I thought was just a stick. I went to pedal, and nothing moved; my cranks were locked up. I stopped and looked back to find my Deore 6100 derailleur twisted up.

![Twisted Deore 6100 Rear Derailleur](/images/xt-linkglide-review/twisted-deore-6100.jpeg)

After looking at prices and deciding what to do, I found a great deal on a Shimano XT Linkglide 8130 groupset. It has Linkglide, which Shimano claims increases drivetrain life; all the items are reasonably priced, and it's Shimano, which is my preference. Another big advantage is that my gravel bike is Shimano GRX 11spd, so I really only need 11spd chains on hand.

## Installation

Installation was a breeze and took minimal effort. I replaced my housing and used the new cable that came with it. Everything had that nice XT feeling. Setup and tuning were much easier than my experiences with Shimano 12spd. I used the same Deore 6100 crank with the HG+ 12spd 30t chainring.

## The Experience

I have been riding this for over two months and 300-plus miles of use. Let me start with the big question: do I miss 12spd? I absolutely don't. 11spd Linkglide has been great. I had to adjust to the small gearing changes, but nothing that a ride didn't sort out. Shifting isn't nearly as fast, but it's very positive. It is more "agricultural" for lack of a better way to say it, which I appreciated. I had read the review by PinkBike, which stated they had used the 12spd chainring, but it was a little noisy. I would agree with that; the first few rides had a lot of noise, and as it has bedded in, the noise has greatly reduced. The one negative of using the 12spd chainring is the "hooks". It's a little clunky if you happen to do any ratcheting as it grabs the chain, but this also happened on 12spd. As far as chain wear goes, it doesn't appear to be showing much. That's already a positive compared to 12spd, which I would have had to replace already.

## Conclusion

I can see any bike I purchase getting 11spd Linkglide as the stock drivetrain wears out and needs replacing. It's solid, durable, and appears to have great longevity. Hopefully, Shimano stays with it and keeps producing the XT-level version.
